# Editorial review

Use this for conceptual, product or strategy films before production and after major narrative revisions. Adapt the questions for demos, tutorials and promotional formats; do not require an architecture diagram in every genre.

## Viewer understanding

From the script alone, describe the central idea in a sentence and sketch the relationship the viewer should remember. If this cannot be done without enumerating every scene, revise the selection and hierarchy. Confirm that the opening provides the context needed to understand the first substantive claim.

Separate material needed for the argument from information merely available in the source. Every factual aside, product name and number consumes attention. Keep it if it establishes the central idea, explains a decision or provides necessary evidence; otherwise move it to accompanying material or omit it.

Compare the proposed model with the original argument and figures. Identify the important relationships retained, changed or lost, including exceptions and alternate paths. A generic diagram with added statistics is not a substitute for this check. When shortening speech, assign specific evidence to visual beats instead of removing the relationships that make the subject distinctive.

## Continuity

Read narration consecutively, ignoring headings. Identify what each paragraph inherits and why its next paragraph follows. Replace title changes and generic promises with an actual logical connection. Ensure the pictures preserve or transform the same concepts when the argument carries them forward.

Check the overall structure. An overview should orient the viewer; the body should develop it; a closing should resolve or apply it. Repeating introductory words without a more useful final model does not create a satisfying conclusion.

## Visual explanation

Review the intended keyframes, rather than accepting the word “architecture” or “overview” in a scene description. Can the image communicate a relationship that speech alone would make hard to follow? Are arrows, timing, optional branches and factual boundaries correct? Does the viewer get enough time to inspect each important change?

Check that examples occupy the same comparison framework. Distinguish existing capabilities from proposals, historical events from inferred strategy, public cases from usage or revenue rankings, and sample successes from long-term service guarantees.

## Outcomes and corrections

Record actionable findings outside the audience-facing film or report. Correct omissions, misleading relationships, excessive information and weak transitions before generating new production assets. Do not replace these corrections with checklist completion, self-scoring or successful export.

When the user requests a script review checkpoint, hand over the revised narration and keyframes. Do not regenerate the film merely to make the review package look finished.
