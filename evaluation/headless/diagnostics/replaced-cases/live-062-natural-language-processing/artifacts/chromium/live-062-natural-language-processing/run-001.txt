**Natural language processing** (**NLP**) is the processing of [natural language](https://ka.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%83%91%E1%83%A3%E1%83%9C%E1%83%94%E1%83%91%E1%83%A0%E1%83%98%E1%83%95%E1%83%98_%E1%83%94%E1%83%9C%E1%83%98%E1%83%A1_%E1%83%93%E1%83%90%E1%83%9B%E1%83%A3%E1%83%A8%E1%83%90%E1%83%95%E1%83%94%E1%83%91%E1%83%90 "ბუნებრივი ენის დამუშავება – Georgian") information by a [computer](ka "ბუნებრივი ენის დამუშავება – Georgian"). NLP is a subfield of [computer science](ka "ბუნებრივი ენის დამუშავება – Georgian") and is closely associated with [artificial intelligence](https://kaa.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1biyiy_tildi_qayta_islew "Tábiyiy tildi qayta islew – Kara-Kalpak"). NLP is also related to [information retrieval](https://kaa.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1biyiy_tildi_qayta_islew "Tábiyiy tildi qayta islew – Kara-Kalpak"), [knowledge representation](kaa "Tábiyiy tildi qayta islew – Kara-Kalpak"), [computational linguistics](https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%B9%E0%B2%9C_%E0%B2%AD%E0%B2%BE%E0%B2%B7%E0%B2%BE_%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%82%E0%B2%B8%E0%B3%8D%E0%B2%95%E0%B2%B0%E0%B2%A3%E0%B3%86 "ಸಹಜ ಭಾಷಾ ಸಂಸ್ಕರಣೆ – Kannada"), and [linguistics](https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%B9%E0%B2%9C_%E0%B2%AD%E0%B2%BE%E0%B2%B7%E0%B2%BE_%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%82%E0%B2%B8%E0%B3%8D%E0%B2%95%E0%B2%B0%E0%B2%A3%E0%B3%86 "ಸಹಜ ಭಾಷಾ ಸಂಸ್ಕರಣೆ – Kannada") more
broadly.[[1]](https://ko.wikipedia.org/wiki/%EC%9E%90%EC%97%B0%EC%96%B4_%EC%B2%98%EB%A6%AC "자연어 처리 – Korean")

Major processing tasks in an NLP system include: [speech recognition](https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat%C5%ABraliosios_kalbos_apdorojimas "자연어 처리"), [text classification](https://lt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat%C5%ABraliosios_kalbos_apdorojimas "Natūraliosios kalbos apdorojimas – Lithuanian"), [natural language understanding](lt "Natūraliosios kalbos apdorojimas – Lithuanian"), and [natural language generation](https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabisk%C4%81s_valodas_apstr%C4%81de "Dabiskās valodas apstrāde – Latvian").


## History
[[edit](https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8_%D1%98%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8 "Обработка на природни јазици –
Macedonian")]

Further information: [History of natural language processing](mnw "အရေဝ်ဘာသာဗေဒ သဘာဝ – Mon")
Natural language processing has its roots in the 1950s.[[2]](mr "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया – Marathi")
Already in 1950, [Alan Turing](mr "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया") published an article titled "[Computing Machinery and Intelligence](https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%80%9E%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9D%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9E%E1%80%AC%E1%80%85%E1%80%80%E1%80%AC%E1%80%B8_%E1%80%85%E1%80%AE%E1%80%99%E1%80%B6%E1%80%86%E1%80%B1%E1%80%AC%E1%80%84%E1%80%BA%E1%80%9B%E1%80%BD%E1%80%80%E1%80%BA%E1%80%99%E1%80%BE%E1%80%AF "သဘာဝဘာသာစကား စီမံဆောင်ရွက်မှု – Burmese")," which proposed what is now called the [Turing test](my "သဘာဝဘာသာစကား စီမံဆောင်ရွက်မှု – Burmese") as a criterion of intelligence, though at the time that was not articulated as a problem separate from artificial intelligence. The proposed test includes a task that involves the automated interpretation and generation of natural language.


### Symbolic NLP (1950s – early 1990s)
[[edit](nl "Taaltechnologie – Dutch")][![](TemplateStyles:r1248332772 "Språkteknologi")](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spr%C3%A5kteknologi "Språkteknologi – Norwegian Bokmål")
A document parsed into an abstract syntax tree
The premise of symbolic NLP is often illustrated using [John Searle's Chinese room](https://pcd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traitemint_automatique_d%27ches_langues "Traitemint automatique d'ches langues – Picard") thought experiment: Given a collection of rules (e.g., a Chinese phrasebook, with questions and matching answers), the computer emulates natural language understanding (or other NLP tasks) by applying those rules to the data it confronts.

- **1950s**: The [Georgetown experiment](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przetwarzanie_j%C4%99zyka_naturalnego "Przetwarzanie języka naturalnego – Polish") in 1954 involved fully [automatic translation](pl "Przetwarzanie języka naturalnego") of more than sixty Russian sentences into English. The authors claimed that within three or five years, machine translation would be a solved problem.[[3]](https://ps.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%AF_%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%8A_%DA%98%D8%A8%DB%90_%D9%BE%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%B3_%DA%A9%D9%88%D9%84 "د طبيعي ژبې پروسس کول – Pashto")  However, real progress was much slower, and after the [ALPAC report](ps "د طبيعي ژبې پروسس کول") in 1966, which found that ten years of research had failed to fulfill the expectations, funding for machine translation was dramatically reduced. Little further research in machine translation was conducted in America (though some research continued elsewhere, such as Japan and Europe[[4]](https://qu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paqariq_simi_thatkichay "Paqariq simi thatkichay – Quechua")) until the late 1980s when the first [statistical machine translation](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelucrarea_limbajului_natural "Prelucrarea limbajului natural – Romanian") systems were developed.
- **1960s**: Some notably successful natural language processing systems developed in the 1960s were [SHRDLU](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%BE_%D1%8F%D0%B7%D1%8B%D0%BA%D0%B0 "Prelucrarea limbajului natural"), a natural language system working in restricted "[blocks worlds](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%BE_%D1%8F%D0%B7%D1%8B%D0%BA%D0%B0 "Обработка естественного языка – Russian")" with restricted vocabularies, and [ELIZA](ru "Обработка естественного языка – Russian"), a simulation of Rogerian psychotherapy, written by [Joseph Weizenbaum](ru "Обработка естественного языка") between 1964 and 1966. Despite using minimal information about human thought or emotion, ELIZA was able to produce interactions that appeared human-like. When the "patient" exceeded the very small knowledge base, ELIZA might provide a generic response, for example, responding to "My head hurts" with "Why do you say your head hurts?". Ross Quillian's successful work on natural language was demonstrated with a vocabulary of only *twenty* words, because that was all that would fit in a computer memory at the time.[[5]](en-simple "Natural language processing")
- **1970s**: During the 1970s, many programmers began to write "conceptual [ontologies](https://sq.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%ABrpunimi_i_gjuh%C3%ABs_natyrore "Përpunimi i gjuhës natyrore – Albanian")", which structured real-world information into computer-understandable data.  Examples are MARGIE (Schank, 1975), SAM (Cullingford, 1978), PAM (Wilensky, 1978), TaleSpin (Meehan, 1976), QUALM (Lehnert, 1977), Politics (Carbonell, 1979), and Plot Units (Lehnert 1981). During this time, the first [chatterbots](sr "Obrada prirodnih jezika – Serbian") were written (e.g., [PARRY](https://ta.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%AE%87%E0%AE%AF%E0%AE%B1%E0%AF%8D%E0%AE%95%E0%AF%88_%E0%AE%AE%E0%AF%8A%E0%AE%B4%E0%AE%BF_%E0%AE%AE%E0%AF%81%E0%AE%B1%E0%AF%88%E0%AE%AF%E0%AE%BE%E0%AE%95%E0%AF%8D%E0%AE%95%E0%AE%AE%E0%AF%8D "இயற்கை மொழி முறையாக்கம் – Tamil")).
- **1980s**: The 1980s and early 1990s mark the heyday of symbolic methods in NLP. Focus areas of the time included research on rule-based parsing (e.g., the development of [HPSG](ta "இயற்கை மொழி முறையாக்கம் – Tamil") as a computational operationalization of [generative grammar](ta "இயற்கை மொழி முறையாக்கம்")), morphology (e.g., two-level morphology[[6]](te "సహజ భాషా ప్రక్రియ – Telugu")), semantics (e.g., [Lesk algorithm](https://th.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B8%81%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%9B%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%B0%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%A7%E0%B8%A5%E0%B8%A0%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%A9%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%98%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%A3%E0%B8%A1%E0%B8%8A%E0%B8%B2%E0%B8%95%E0%B8%B4 "การประมวลภาษาธรรมชาติ – Thai")), reference (e.g., within Centering Theory[[7]](th "การประมวลภาษาธรรมชาติ – Thai")) and other areas of natural language understanding (e.g., in the [Rhetorical Structure Theory](/w/load.php?lang=en&modules=ext.cite.parsoid.styles%7Cext.cite.styles%7Cext.math.styles%7Cext.uls.interlanguage%7Cext.visualEditor.desktopArticleTarget.noscript%7Cext.wikimediaBadges%7Cext.wikimediaBadges.ulsV2%7Cext.wikimediamessages.styles%7Cjquery.makeCollapsible.styles%7Cmediawiki.skinning.content.parsoid%7Cskins.vector.icons%2Cstyles%7Cskins.vector.search.codex.styles%7Cwikibase.client.init&only=styles&skin=vector-2022 "client-js vector-feature-language-in-header-enabled vector-feature-language-in-main-menu-disabled vector-feature-language-in-main-page-header-disabled vector-feature-page-tools-pinned-disabled vector-feature-toc-pinned-clientpref-1 vector-feature-main-menu-pinned-disabled vector-feature-limited-width-clientpref-1 vector-feature-limited-width-content-enabled vector-feature-custom-font-size-clientpref-1 vector-feature-appearance-pinned-clientpref-1 skin-theme-clientpref-day vector-sticky-header-enabled vector-toc-available skin-thumbsize-clientpref-standard")). Up until the 1980s, most natural language processing systems were based on complex
sets of hand-written rules. Starting in the late 1980s, however, there was a revolution
in natural language processing with the introduction of [machine learning](/w/load.php?lang=en&modules=ext.cite.parsoid.styles%7Cext.cite.styles%7Cext.math.styles%7Cext.uls.interlanguage%7Cext.visualEditor.desktopArticleTarget.noscript%7Cext.wikimediaBadges%7Cext.wikimediaBadges.ulsV2%7Cext.wikimediamessages.styles%7Cjquery.makeCollapsible.styles%7Cmediawiki.skinning.content.parsoid%7Cskins.vector.icons%2Cstyles%7Cskins.vector.search.codex.styles%7Cwikibase.client.init&only=styles&skin=vector-2022 "ext.globalCssJs.user.styles") algorithms for language processing. This shift was influenced by increasing computational power (see [Moore's law](/w/load.php?lang=en&modules=ext.cite.parsoid.styles%7Cext.cite.styles%7Cext.math.styles%7Cext.uls.interlanguage%7Cext.visualEditor.desktopArticleTarget.noscript%7Cext.wikimediaBadges%7Cext.wikimediaBadges.ulsV2%7Cext.wikimediamessages.styles%7Cjquery.makeCollapsible.styles%7Cmediawiki.skinning.content.parsoid%7Cskins.vector.icons%2Cstyles%7Cskins.vector.search.codex.styles%7Cwikibase.client.init&only=styles&skin=vector-2022 "Natural language processing - Wikipedia")) and a decline in the dominance of [Chomskyan](/w/load.php?lang=en&modules=ext.cite.parsoid.styles%7Cext.cite.styles%7Cext.math.styles%7Cext.uls.interlanguage%7Cext.visualEditor.desktopArticleTarget.noscript%7Cext.wikimediaBadges%7Cext.wikimediaBadges.ulsV2%7Cext.wikimediamessages.styles%7Cjquery.makeCollapsible.styles%7Cmediawiki.skinning.content.parsoid%7Cskins.vector.icons%2Cstyles%7Cskins.vector.search.codex.styles%7Cwikibase.client.init&only=styles&skin=vector-2022 "Natural language processing - ## Approaches: Symbolic, statistical, neural networks
[[edit](/wiki/Special:Search "Search Wikipedia [f]")]
Symbolic approach, i.e., the hand-coding of a set of rules for manipulating symbols,
coupled with a dictionary lookup, was historically the first approach used both by
AI in general and by NLP in particular:[[22]](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "Search Wikipedia
[f]")[[23]](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "Change the appearance of the page's font size, width, and color")
such as by writing grammars or devising heuristic rules for [stemming](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "Change the appearance of the page's font size, width, and color").

[Machine learning](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "Change the appearance of the page's font size, width, and color") approaches, which include both statistical and neural networks, on the other hand, have many advantages over the symbolic approach:

- both statistical and neural network methods tend to focus more on the most common cases extracted from a corpus of texts, whereas the rule-based approach needs to provide rules for both rare and common cases equally.
- [language models](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en " title="), produced by either statistical or neural network methods, are more robust to both unfamiliar (e.g. containing words or structures that have not been seen before) and erroneous input (e.g. with misspelled words or words accidentally omitted) in comparison to the rule-based systems, which are also more costly to produce.
- the larger such a (probabilistic) language model is, the more accurate it becomes, in contrast to rule-based systems that can gain accuracy only by increasing the amount and complexity of the rules leading to [intractability](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en " title=") problems.

Rule-based systems are commonly used:

- when the amount of training data is insufficient to successfully apply machine learning methods, e.g., for the machine translation of low-resource languages such as provided by the [Apertium](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en " title=") system,
- for preprocessing in NLP pipelines, e.g., [tokenization](/w/index.php?title=Special:CreateAccount&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title="), or
- for post-processing and transforming the output of NLP pipelines, e.g., for [knowledge extraction](/w/index.php?title=Special:UserLogin&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title=") from syntactic parses.


### Statistical approach
[[edit](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "Log in and more
options")]
In the late 1980s and mid-1990s, the statistical approach ended a period of [AI winter](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "User menu"), which was caused by the inefficiencies of the rule-based
approaches.[[24]](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "User
menu")[[25]](/w/index.php?title=Special:CreateAccount&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title=")

The earliest [decision trees](/w/index.php?title=Special:UserLogin&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title="), producing systems of hard [if–then rules](/w/index.php?title=Special:UserLogin&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title="), were still very similar to the old rule-based approaches.Only the introduction of hidden [Markov models](# " title="), applied to part-of-speech tagging, announced the end of the old rule-based approach.


### Neural networks
[[edit](# "Contents")]

Further information: [Artificial neural network](# "Contents")
A major drawback of statistical methods is that they require elaborate [feature engineering](# "Contents"). Since
2015,[[26]](#History "Contents") [neural network](#History "Contents")–based methods have increasingly replaced traditional statistical approaches, using [semantic networks](#Symbolic_NLP_(1950s_–_early_1990s)
"Contents")[[27]](#Statistical_NLP_(1990s–present) "Contents") and [word embeddings](#Statistical_NLP_(1990s–present) "Contents") to capture semantic properties of words.

Intermediate tasks (e.g., part-of-speech tagging and dependency parsing) are not
needed anymore.

[Neural machine translation](#Approaches:_Symbolic,_statistical,_neural_networks "Contents"), based on the then-newly invented [sequence-to-sequence](#Approaches:_Symbolic,_statistical,_neural_networks "Contents") transformations, made obsolete the intermediate steps, such as word alignment, previously necessary for [statistical machine translation](#Statistical_approach "Contents").


## Common NLP tasks

The following is a list of some of the most commonly researched tasks in natural
language processing. Some of these tasks have direct real-world applications, while
others more commonly serve as subtasks that are used to aid in solving larger tasks.

Though natural language processing tasks are closely intertwined, they can be subdivided
into categories for convenience. A coarse division is given below.

(*i.e.*, the structure of words) of the language being considered. [English](https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%BE%D1%9E%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%8B "Апрацоўка натуральнай мовы – Belarusian") has fairly simple morphology, especially [inflectional morphology](https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%BE%D1%9E%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%8B "Апрацоўка натуральнай мовы – Belarusian"), and thus it is often possible to ignore this task entirely and simply model all possible forms of a word (e.g., "open, opens, opened, opening") as separate words. In languages such as [Turkish](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD_%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BA "Обработка на естествен език – Bulgarian") or [Meitei](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD_%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BA "Обработка на естествен език – Bulgarian"), a highly [agglutinated](bg "Обработка на естествен език – Bulgarian") Indian language, however, such an approach is not possible, as each dictionary entry has thousands of possible word
forms.[[33]](https://bn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A6%B8%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AD%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%AC%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%95_%E0%A6%AD%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%B7%E0%A6%BE_%E0%A6%AA%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%95%E0%A7%8D%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%BF%E0%A6%AF%E0%A6%BC%E0%A6%BE%E0%A6%95%E0%A6%B0%E0%A6%A3 "স্বাভাবিক ভাষা প্রক্রিয়াকরণ – Bangla")[Part-of-speech tagging](bn "স্বাভাবিক ভাষা প্রক্রিয়াকরণ –
Bangla")Given a sentence, determine the [part of speech](bn "স্বাভাবিক ভাষা প্রক্রিয়াকরণ") (POS) for each word. Many words, especially common ones, can serve as multiple parts of speech. For example, "book" can be a [noun](https://br.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treterezh_emgefre_al_lavar "Treterezh emgefre al lavar – Breton") ("the book on the table") or [verb](br "Treterezh emgefre al lavar – Breton") ("to book a flight"); "set" can be a noun, verb or [adjective](https://bs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obrada_prirodnog_jezika "Obrada prirodnog jezika – Bosnian"); and "out" can be any of at least five different parts of speech.[Stemming](bs "Obrada prirodnog jezika –
Bosnian")The process of reducing inflected (or sometimes derived) words to a base
form (e.g., "close" will be the root for "closed", "closing", "close", "closer" etc.).
Stemming yields similar results as lemmatization, but does so on grounds of rules,
not a dictionary.

### Lexical semantics (of individual words in context)
[[edit](https://kn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%B9%E0%B2%9C_%E0%B2%AD%E0%B2%BE%E0%B2%B7%E0%B2%BE_%E0%B2%B8%E0%B2%82%E0%B2%B8%E0%B3%8D%E0%B2%95%E0%B2%B0%E0%B2%A3%E0%B3%86 "ಸಹಜ ಭಾಷಾ ಸಂಸ್ಕರಣೆ –
Kannada")][![Taaltechnologie – Dutch](TemplateStyles:r1248332772 "ಸಹಜ ಭಾಷಾ ಸಂಸ್ಕರಣೆ")](kn "ಸಹಜ ಭಾಷಾ ಸಂಸ್ಕರಣೆ – Kannada")
An entity linking pipeline[Lexical semantics](lt "Natūraliosios kalbos apdorojimas")What
is the computational meaning of individual words in context?[Distributional semantics](https://lv.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabisk%C4%81s_valodas_apstr%C4%81de "Dabiskās valodas apstrāde –
Latvian")How can we learn semantic representations from data?[Named entity recognition](https://mk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%BD%D0%B8_%D1%98%D0%B0%D0%B7%D0%B8%D1%86%D0%B8 "Обработка на природни јазици – Macedonian")
(NER)Given a stream of text, determine which items in the text map to proper names,
such as people or places, and what the type of each such name is (e.g. person, location,
organization). Although [capitalization](mk "Обработка на природни јазици") can aid in recognizing named entities in languages such as English, this information cannot aid in determining the type of [named entity](https://mn.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9A%D0%BE%D0%BC%D0%BF%D1%8C%D1%8E%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%80%D1%8B%D0%BD_%D1%85%D1%8D%D0%BB_%D1%88%D0%B8%D0%BD%D0%B6%D0%BB%D1%8D%D0%BB "Компьютерын хэл шинжлэл – Mongolian"), and in any case, is often inaccurate or insufficient.  For example, the first letter of a sentence is also capitalized, and named entities often span several words, only some of which are capitalized.  Furthermore, many other languages in non-Western scripts (e.g. [Chinese](https://mnw.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%80%A1%E1%80%9B%E1%80%B1%E1%80%9D%E1%80%BA%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9E%E1%80%AC%E1%80%97%E1%80%B1%E1%80%92_%E1%80%9E%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9D "အရေဝ်ဘာသာဗေဒ သဘာဝ – Mon") or [Arabic](https://mnw.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%80%A1%E1%80%9B%E1%80%B1%E1%80%9D%E1%80%BA%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9E%E1%80%AC%E1%80%97%E1%80%B1%E1%80%92_%E1%80%9E%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9D "အရေဝ်ဘာသာဗေဒ သဘာဝ – Mon")) do not have any capitalization at all, and even languages with capitalization may not consistently use it to distinguish names. For example, [German](mnw "အရေဝ်ဘာသာဗေဒ သဘာဝ – Mon") capitalizes all [nouns](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%88%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95_%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BE_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया – Marathi"), regardless of whether they are names, and [French](https://mr.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%A4%A8%E0%A5%88%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95_%E0%A4%AD%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B7%E0%A4%BE_%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%BE "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया – Marathi") and [Spanish](mr "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया – Marathi") do not capitalize names that serve as [adjectives](mr "नैसर्गिक भाषा प्रक्रिया – Marathi"). This task is also referred to as token
classification.[[35]](https://my.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E1%80%9E%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9D%E1%80%98%E1%80%AC%E1%80%9E%E1%80%AC%E1%80%85%E1%80%80%E1%80%AC%E1%80%B8_%E1%80%85%E1%80%AE%E1%80%99%E1%80%B6%E1%80%86%E1%80%B1%E1%80%AC%E1%80%84%E1%80%BA%E1%80%9B%E1%80%BD%E1%80%80%E1%80%BA%E1%80%99%E1%80%BE%E1%80%AF "သဘာဝဘာသာစကား စီမံဆောင်ရွက်မှု – Burmese")[Sentiment analysis](my "သဘာဝဘာသာစကား စီမံဆောင်ရွက်မှု – Burmese") (see also [Multimodal sentiment analysis](my "သဘာဝဘာသာစကား
စီမံဆောင်ရွက်မှု"))Sentiment analysis involves identifying
and classifying the emotional tone expressed in text. This technique involves analyzing
text to determine whether the expressed sentiment is positive, negative, or neutral.
Models for sentiment classification typically utilize inputs such as [word n-grams](nl "Taaltechnologie"), [Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency](https://no.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spr%C3%A5kteknologi "Språkteknologi – Norwegian Bokmål") (TF-IDF) features, hand-generated features, or employ [deep learning](https://or.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%AC%A8%E0%AD%8D%E0%AD%9F%E0%AC%BE%E0%AC%9A%E0%AD%81%E0%AC%B0%E0%AC%BE%E0%AC%B2_%E0%AC%B2%E0%AC%BE%E0%AC%99%E0%AD%8D%E0%AC%97%E0%AD%81%E0%AC%8F%E0%AC%9C_%E0%AC%AA%E0%AD%8D%E0%AC%B0%E0%AD%8B%E0%AC%B8%E0%AD%87%E0%AC%B8%E0%AC%BF%E0%AC%82 "ନ୍ୟାଚୁରାଲ ଲାଙ୍ଗୁଏଜ ପ୍ରୋସେସିଂ – Odia") models designed to recognize both long-term and short-term dependencies in text sequences. The applications of sentiment analysis are diverse, extending to tasks such as categorizing customer reviews on various online
platforms.[[29]](https://pcd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traitemint_automatique_d%27ches_langues "Traitemint automatique d'ches langues – Picard")[Terminology extraction](https://pcd.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traitemint_automatique_d%27ches_langues "Traitemint automatique d'ches langues –
Picard")The goal of terminology extraction is to automatically extract relevant
terms from a given corpus.[Word-sense disambiguation](https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Przetwarzanie_j%C4%99zyka_naturalnego "Przetwarzanie języka naturalnego – Polish")
(WSD)Many words have more than one [meaning](pl "Przetwarzanie języka naturalnego – Polish"); we have to select the meaning which makes the most sense in context.  For this problem, we are typically given a list of words and associated word senses, e.g. from a dictionary or an online resource such as [WordNet](pms "NLP – Piedmontese").[Entity linking](https://ps.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%AF_%D8%B7%D8%A8%D9%8A%D8%B9%D9%8A_%DA%98%D8%A8%DB%90_%D9%BE%D8%B1%D9%88%D8%B3%D8%B3_%DA%A9%D9%88%D9%84 "د طبيعي ژبې پروسس کول –
Pashto")Many words—typically proper names—refer to [named entities](ps "د طبيعي ژبې پروسس کول – Pashto"); here we have to select the entity (a famous individual, a location, a company, etc.) which is referred to in context.


### Relational semantics (semantics of individual sentences)
[[edit](https://qu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paqariq_simi_thatkichay "Paqariq simi thatkichay –
Quechua")][Relationship extraction](https://ro.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prelucrarea_limbajului_natural "Prelucrarea limbajului natural –
Romanian")Given a chunk of text, identify the relationships among named entities
(e.g. who is married to whom).[Semantic parsing](ro "Prelucrarea limbajului natural")Given
a piece of text (typically a sentence), produce a formal representation of its semantics,
either as a graph (e.g., in [AMR parsing](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B3%D0%BE_%D1%8F%D0%B7%D1%8B%D0%BA%D0%B0 "Обработка естественного языка – Russian")) or in accordance with a logical formalism (e.g., in [DRT parsing](ru "Обработка естественного языка – Russian")). This challenge typically includes aspects of several more elementary NLP tasks from semantics (e.g., semantic role labelling, word-sense disambiguation) and can be extended to include full-fledged discourse analysis (e.g., discourse analysis, coreference; see [Natural language understanding](sh "Obrada prirodnih jezika – Serbo-Croatian") below).[Semantic role labelling](https://simple.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_language_processing "Natural language processing – Simple English") (see also implicit semantic role labelling
below)Given a single sentence, identify and disambiguate semantic predicates (e.g.,

### Discourse (semantics beyond individual sentences)

## General tendencies and (possible) future directions
[[edit](https://donate.wikimedia.org/?wmf_source=donate&wmf_medium=sidebar&wmf_campaign=en.wikipedia.org&uselang=en "
title=")]
Based on long-standing trends in the field, it is possible to extrapolate future
directions of NLP. As of 2020, three trends among the topics of the long-standing
series of CoNLL Shared Tasks can be observed:[[52]](/w/index.php?title=Special:UserLogin&returnto=Natural+language+processing " title=")

- Interest in increasingly abstract, "cognitive" aspects of natural language (1999–2001: shallow parsing, 2002–03: named entity recognition, 2006–09/2017–18: dependency syntax, 2004–05/2008–09 semantic role labelling, 2011–12 coreference, 2015–16: discourse parsing, 2019: semantic parsing).
- Increasing interest in multilinguality, and, potentially, multimodality (English since 1999; Spanish, Dutch since 2002; German since 2003; Bulgarian, Danish, Japanese, Portuguese, Slovenian, Swedish, Turkish since 2006; Basque, Catalan, Chinese, Greek, Hungarian, Italian, Turkish since 2007; Czech since 2009; Arabic since 2012; 2017: 40+ languages; 2018: 60+/100+ languages)
- Elimination of symbolic representations (rule-based over supervised towards weakly supervised methods, representation learning and end-to-end systems)


### Cognition
[[edit](# "Contents")]
Most higher-level NLP applications involve aspects that emulate intelligent behavior
and apparent comprehension of natural language. More broadly speaking, the technical
operationalization of increasingly advanced aspects of cognitive behavior represents
one of the developmental trajectories of NLP(see trends among CoNLL shared tasks
above).

[Cognition](# "Contents") refers to "the mental action or process of acquiring knowledge and understanding through thought, experience, and the
senses."[[53]](#History "Contents") [Cognitive science](#Symbolic_NLP_(1950s_–_early_1990s) "Contents") is the interdisciplinary, scientific study of the mind and its
processes.[[54]](#Symbolic_NLP_(1950s_–_early_1990s) "Contents") [Cognitive linguistics](#Statistical_NLP_(1990s–present) "Contents") is an interdisciplinary branch of linguistics, combining knowledge and research from both psychology and
linguistics.[[55]](#Approaches:_Symbolic,_statistical,_neural_networks "Contents")
Especially during the age of [symbolic NLP](#Approaches:_Symbolic,_statistical,_neural_networks "Contents"), the area of computational linguistics maintained strong ties with cognitive studies.

As an example, [George Lakoff](#Statistical_approach "Contents") offers a methodology to build natural language processing (NLP) algorithms through the perspective of cognitive science, along with the findings of cognitive
linguistics,[[56]](#Statistical_approach "Contents") with two defining aspects:

1. Apply the theory of [conceptual metaphor](#Neural_networks "Contents"), explained by Lakoff as "the understanding of one idea, in terms of another" which provides an idea of the intent of the author.[[57]](#Common_NLP_tasks "Contents") For example, consider the English word *big*. When used in a comparison ("That is a big tree"), the author intends to imply that the tree is *physically large* relative to other trees or the author's experience. When used metaphorically ("Tomorrow is a big day"), the author intends to imply *importance*. The intent behind other usages, like in "She is a big person", will remain somewhat ambiguous to a person and a cognitive NLP algorithm alike without additional information.
2. Assign relative measures of meaning to a word, phrase, sentence or piece of text based on the information presented before and after the piece of text being analyzed, e.g., by means of a [probabilistic context-free grammar](#Morphological_analysis "Contents") (PCFG). The mathematical equation for such algorithms is presented in [US Patent 9269353](#Syntactic_analysis "Contents"):[[58]](#Lexical_semantics_(of_individual_words_in_context) "Contents")

Likewise, ideas of cognitive NLP are inherent to neural models [multimodal](https://be.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%90%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%86%D0%BE%D1%9E%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%82%D1%83%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%B0%D0%B9_%D0%BC%D0%BE%D0%B2%D1%8B "Апрацоўка натуральнай мовы – Belarusian") NLP (although rarely made
explicit)[[64]](https://bg.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9E%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%82%D0%BA%D0%B0_%D0%BD%D0%B0_%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%B5%D0%BD_%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B8%D0%BA "Обработка на естествен език – Bulgarian")
and developments in [artificial intelligence](bg "Обработка на естествен език – Bulgarian"), specifically tools and technologies using [large language model](bg "Обработка на естествен език")
