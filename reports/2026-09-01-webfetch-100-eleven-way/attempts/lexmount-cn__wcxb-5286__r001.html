We only rate 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organizations — these are US-based organizations commonly referred to as public charities that work all around the world. With a few exceptions, organizations must have an Accountability & Finance score or an Impact program evaluation to be eligible for a Zero to Four-Star rating.

Our site also includes basic information on charities that don't meet our rating eligibility criteria. Our goal is to give you as much information as possible to guide your giving decisions.

Charities can earn ratings by logging into the Charity Navigator Nonprofit Portal and providing the necessary data.

Eligible charities receive a zero to four-star star rating — this is determined by the weighted sum of the organization’s individual beacon scores (0-100).

Rating Score Assessment Description
90+ Great Exceeds or meets best practices and industry standards across almost all areas. Likely to be a highly-effective charity.
75 - 89 Good Exceeds or meets best practices and industry standards across some areas.
60 - 74 Needs improvement Meets or nearly meets industry standards in a few areas and underperforms most charities.
50 - 59 Poor Fails to meet industry standards in most areas and underperforms almost all charities.
< 50 Very poor Performs below industry standards and well underperforms nearly all charities.

We recognize that not all metrics and beacons equally predict a charity’s success. The percentage each beacon contributes to the organization’s overall rating depends on the number of beacons an organization has earned.

Charities can earn beacons by logging into the Charity Navigator Nonprofit Portal and providing the necessary data.

Providing Guidance Through Beacons

Our Encompass Rating System™ provides a comprehensive analysis of charity performance across four key domains, which we call "beacons." These beacons and their individual ratings can be seen for each of the charities you search. Learn more about each beacon below.

Eligibility criteria We only rate 501(c)(3) tax-exempt organizations — these are US-based organizations commonly referred to as public charities that work all around the world. To receive an Encompass Rating, a charity must have three years of IRS Form 990 filings, which we receive directly from the IRS. For a complete Encompass Rating, charities must also submit data through the Charity Navigator Nonprofit Portal . We do not evaluate private foundations. Our site also includes basic information on charities that don’t meet our rating eligibility criteria. Our goal is to give you as much information as possible to guide your giving decisions. What the ratings mean Eligible charities receive a zero to four-star star rating — this is determined by the weighted sum of the organization’s metrics (0-100). Rating Score Assessment Description 90+ Great Exceeds or meets best practices and industry standards across almost all evaluation areas included in the charity’s rating. 75 – 89 Good Exceeds or meets best practices and industry standards across some evaluation areas included in the charity’s rating. 60 – 74 Needs improvement Meets or nearly meets industry standards in a few evaluation areas included in the charity’s rating and under performs most charities. 50 – 59 Poor Fails to meet industry standards in most evaluation areas included in the charity’s rating and underperforms almost all charities. < 50 Very poor Performs below industry standards as reflected in the charity’s rating and well underperforms nearly all charities. Providing Guidance Through Beacons Our Encompass Rating System™ provides a comprehensive analysis of charity performance across four key domains, which we call “beacons.” Learn more about each beacon below. We continually evaluate and build upon our methodology to strengthen how we assess charity effectiveness and efficiency.
