# News ProductManagement Certification

Advance your career with the technical and product expertise to lead strategic decision-making, bridge audience needs with business sustainability, and inspire change in the news industry.

in partnership with

The NPMC is a rigorous, part-time, virtual program for mid- to senior-level news professionals seeking the product strategy, technical expertise, and change management skills needed to create value for their audiences and organizations. As the industry’s first certification dedicated to news product management, we have successfully trained over 150 alumni across 35 countries, creating a consolidated community of practitioners who are currently redefining newsrooms at The New York Times, Reuters, Lookout Local, NPR, The 19th, BBC, Der Spiegel, and more.

Our curriculum is built specifically around the realities of news organizations and the unique pressures, constraints, and opportunities that define product work in journalism.

The program takes you from strategy to execution, covering everything you need to thrive in a news product leadership role. You'll learn how to identify audience needs and business opportunities and transform them into actionable insights, as well as how to develop and execute a product strategy. From there, we dive into technical skill-building: covering data management, user experience, and aligning the tech, design, and engineering sides of things, alongside developing revenue strategies and sustainability. Finally, the program rounds out with product leadership skills: how to lead cross-functional teams, sharpen your communication, articulate your product vision, and work effectively with key stakeholders. Throughout every module, we examine how AI is reshaping product workflows and what it means for the role, with a clear focus on the strategic thinking, judgment, and human expertise that no tool can replace.

Every lesson is taught by senior practitioners actively working across tech and news — from organizations like The Atlantic, Netflix, Chicago Public Media, and Yahoo News — ensuring the curriculum stays grounded in what's actually happening in the field. Past sessions have included:

— Ensuring Revenue Sustainability with a Clear Monetization Strategy: Analyzing core and complementary revenue models to help participants evaluate and implement the approaches that best fit their product's mission.

— Navigating AI as a Product Leader: Identifying opportunities to integrate AI into product workflows and developing foundational technical knowledge to support collaboration on AI-powered products.

— Aligning Product Strategy with Tech and Engineering Capabilities: Learn to bridge the gap between product management and engineering teams by understanding engineering processes, terminology, and constraints.

— Navigating Newsroom Culture and Managing Stakeholders: Developing strategies to improve product execution by effectively communicating with diverse stakeholders.

This blend of expertise ensures that participants gain a baseline of technical knowledge immediately applicable to the unique constraints of the news industry.

Those who successfully complete this program will:

— Master key product development and management methodologies used in top newsrooms and technology companies globally.

— Apply product management frameworks to real-life case studies from news organizations.

— Effectively manage cross-departmental stakeholders and lead strategic decision-making.

— Navigate AI with the strategic and ethical frameworks necessary to integrate emerging technology into news products and discovery.

### Apply for the NPMC program!

Each cohort participates for 3.5 months in weekly live virtual sessions with experienced news product professionals. They also have access to reading materials, instructor-led discussions, and coaching sessions with experts. At the end of the program, participants present a final project that showcases their ability to lead strategic decision-making for a news product and receive a certificate of completion.

### FAQ

1. Who is this program for?

This course is for mid- to senior-level news professionals who are passionate about solving complex problems in the news industry with product solutions. We are looking for candidates who have some product experience from working on editorial products, and may work in mid-level or senior roles from any part of the organization, including editorial, technology, audience and revenue positions.

This program is for you if you aim to:

Transition from an editorial or other role into a formal product role.

Improve your product strategy and lead your team to strategic outcomes.

Lead with influence and manage change across a news organization .

Deepen your technical understanding of digital products.

Increase your earning potential and pursue higher leadership roles.

Solidify your product management knowledge and build confidence in skills you’ve learned on the job.

Learn from industry experts and network with peers in the field

2. Is this an entry-level program?

While you do not need to be in a leadership or management position to participate in this program, the NPMC is not an entry-level program.

We are looking for participants who have some experience working in product development or product strategy and are prepared to lead through influence. For example, you worked on an email newsletter and had to consider monetization or subscription strategies; you worked on the ad sales team and researched new programming solutions when your organization introduced a paywall product; or you worked with editorial stakeholders to incorporate new features into the development of a content management system; among other possible scenarios.

3. What is expected of participants?

The expected time commitment is 3.5 months consists of an estimated 7-10 hours of studying and project work per week.

Each week, participants will participate in live sessions, and they will be given reading materials to review, and other resources. Instructors will host live virtual sessions to review the learning materials and lead a group activity, such as a discussion or interactive exercise. Participants will also work with coaches to produce a final capstone project that showcases their ability to lead strategic decision-making for a news product.

We ask participants to show up prepared to actively engage with group activities and follow our code of conduct to maintain a safe and inclusive learning environment.

4. What does the curriculum include?

Our curriculum is informed by in-depth interviews with hiring managers from the NPA Community and successful news product teams, including those at The New York Times, The Atlantic, New York Public Radio, CalMatters, and more. We also gathered community feedback through our Mentor Network program and our exclusive case studies about news organizations developed by the NPA.

The beginning of the course will strengthen your foundation of product management knowledge. Next, we’ll dive deeper into technical skill building with sessions to help participants improve hard skills, such as data analysis and technical communication. And lastly, we’ll cover how to integrate these skills and apply them to develop strategic solutions and lead with influence in your news organization.

5. Do I have to prepare a final project?

Yes, all selected participants will develop and present a product strategy. The goal of this project is to practice and hone the key skills you need for each stage of the product management cycle and how to communicate ideas effectively. Participants will start by identifying a problem or opportunity to focus on, then work to gain the insights necessary to make informed decisions. Next, they’ll synthesize those insights into possible solutions and a cohesive product strategy, so that they can communicate the ideas in a persuasive presentation.

The focus of the final project is at every participant’s discretion. Examples include pitching a new product strategy to fill an audience gap, pivoting the strategy or adjusting features of an existing product, and sunsetting a product or feature that no longer meets the needs of the organization.

6. Who teaches the course?

Our core curriculum will be led by News Product Alliance team leaders, and we will invite expert practitioners from across the media and tech industry to join us as instructors and speakers, elevating the experience of the Cohort by bringing outside perspectives and deep expertise on key topics.

Our potential instructor list includes a mix of well-known and emerging voices, bringing substantial and diverse perspectives to our curriculum, including instructors and coaches from NPMC Cohort 1, Cohort 2, Cohort 3, and Cohort 4.

7. Will the course sessions be viewable on demand or asynchronously?

As this is a certification program, participants are expected to be actively engaged, which includes attendance at virtual sessions. Some course materials like lectures and readings will be provided for participants to prepare in advance. However, live attendance and participation during the sessions are required to ensure a full and engaging cohort experience.

8. What is the course schedule?

The classes will take place on Thursdays between the hours of 11 a.m to 1 p.m. Eastern Time. Additionally, Peer Circle sessions will be held every other Friday from 11 a.m. to 12 p.m. ET

9. How much does the program cost?

The total cost is $5,500, which includes tuition, coaching, learning materials, and exclusive access to networking opportunities. We offer the option to make payments in installments.

10. Is tuition assistance available?

A limited number of full and partial financial support will be available. This process will not affect the application process and the financial support decisions will be made after being accepted to the program.

11. Can my newsroom reserve multiple seats in advance?

Companies can secure spots for team members by committing to participate in the program. We only allow a maximum of two participants from the same newsroom in each cohort to ensure a diverse cohort experience. All participants will still need to submit an application. If your newsroom is interested in sending people to the program, please reach out to npmc@newsproduct.org for more information.

12. What do participants get upon completion of the program?

Upon completion of the program, participants will receive a certificate of completion. This can be used to signal to employers that you’ve completed a rigorous training program and are equipped with product management skills.

13. Is the program open to international applicants?

Yes! We welcome diverse perspectives from around the world in this virtual program. We highly encourage our global community of news product professionals to apply.

14. Do I need to know the exact area of product management I want to pursue before applying to the program?

No, it's not necessary to know the exact area of product management you want to pursue before applying. The motivation to develop your toolkit for leading product management processes is enough.

15. Are applications available for those who don't work in the news media industry?

The NPMC is focused on professionals who work in the media industry or have a background in media, and demonstrated experience and interest in journalism. However, the program can be valuable to other content-adjacent organizations and participants who aim to transition to a product role in the news industry. We encourage all who are interested to apply and reach out to our team if specific questions arise.

16. Can I apply to the program independently, without being affiliated with a newsroom?

Yes, the program is suitable for individuals from diverse backgrounds, including freelancers, those working in newsrooms, or those working on products affecting the news industry. Individuals unaffiliated with a newsroom can apply independently. The program aims to develop leadership in product management within the news industry.

17. What does the application process entail?

As a first step, applicants must complete an application form and answer questions about why they're interested in this program, provide a resume and the contact information for two references, among other requirements.
